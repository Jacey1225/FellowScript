# Research Brief: FellowScript Gatekeeper Outreach Tactics

## Summary

This brief answers the two open questions carried forward from the prior task (`20260814-fellowscript-marketing`): (1) whether Hallow's/YouVersion's institutional parish-partnership tactics transfer to a smaller, group-study-centric product or need a different leader-first shape, and (2) whether campus-ministry-coalition partnerships are worth pursuing given FellowScript's small-team constraints. It is organized around the plan's eight research steps.

### 1. What the prior research already establishes (background, not re-derived)

The prior task's `brief.md` and `conclusion.md` (read as required first-step background) establish: pastor/leader outreach and "best-of" list placement (e.g., TheLeadPastor) as a real, targetable discovery channel; campus-ministry coalition apps (EveryCampus, 100+ campus ministries at the time of that research) as a live precedented model; a caution from evangelical campus-outreach practitioners that relational/on-the-ground tactics outperform broad digital advertising specifically in the campus-ministry sub-audience; FellowScript's group-priced tiering as the reason group-level gatekeepers are a distinct channel; and Hallow's institutional model (2,500+ parish partnerships, ~9% QR-code-attributed growth) as a directional pattern only — the "30% user increase" figure tied to 2024 Catholic-org collaborations was debunked and is not re-cited here [prior brief.md; prior conclusion.md]. FellowScript's small-team status is likewise a carried-forward constraint, not re-researched [prior brief.md].

### 2. Concrete outreach tactics for reaching pastors and small-group leaders directly

Three tactics recur across independently-motivated sources: (a) **free-tier/pilot offers as a foot-in-the-door** — Text In Church explicitly recommends pilot/trial proposals to work around slow church decision-making cycles [Text In Church]; SmartGroups, FellowScript's closest direct comparable, structurally mirrors this with a free-forever single-group tier plus a "90-Day Pilot" program [SmartGroups About]; agile-ed's champion-enablement material also treats no-cost pilots as the mechanism that creates an internal advocate [Agile Education Marketing]. (b) **Hands-on, in-person first-touch onboarding** — ChurchTechToday recommends leaders introduce the app live at a group's first meeting, with the group downloading it together as a shared activity, rather than a cold digital push [ChurchTechToday]. (c) **Champion identification via existing networks** — agile-ed describes sourcing champions through conference networking, LinkedIn outreach, and educator-community engagement, a pattern structurally transferable to sourcing group-leader champions through ministry networks [Agile Education Marketing]. Notably, no source in this round supplied a literal cold-outreach email/DM script template for pastors or group leaders specifically — this is a gap (see Risks).

### 3. Pitch angles by gatekeeper role

Coverage here is asymmetric, as the plan anticipated. **Small-group leaders**: the clearest, most tactical material — position the app as the group's central hub (agendas, discussion guides, calendars) rather than a bolt-on feature, and sustain engagement via periodic leader check-ins and troubleshooting support through the season [ChurchTechToday]. **Pastors**: Text In Church recommends segmenting the pitch by decision-maker style (mission-focused, strategy-oriented, or data-driven) and leading with concrete-outcome benefits, case studies/testimonials, and cost-benefit framing as an affordable multi-ministry tool [Text In Church]. A dedicated pastoral-technology source recommends framing adoption as serving rather than replacing core pastoral ministry ("Word and Sacrament"), plus transparency about rollout imperfections and one-on-one conversations for resistant individuals — but this same source treats "pastors and church leaders" as one undifferentiated audience, which is itself evidence that pastor-specific (as opposed to generic-leadership) pitch literature is thin [Concordia Technology]. **Ministry coordinators**: the thinnest coverage of the four roles — one source confirms the role (recruitment/training oversight of small-group leaders and other volunteers, distinct from "pastor") but no source supplied coordinator-targeted pitch angles [Guideflow]. **Diocese/parish administrators**: available material skews toward compliance and liability rather than persuasion — diocesan-level offices hold formal, documented responsibility for data-protection compliance, with named oversight roles, and parish-level tool adoption is expected to be checked against diocesan guidance [Diocese of Portsmouth policy; Diocese of Hereford/Newcastle-area guide]. Both compliance sources are UK Anglican/GDPR documents, a regime-mismatch caveat for a likely US-inflected audience (see Risks). Institution-scale rollout more generally follows a needs-assessment → stakeholder-workshop → vendor-comparison → budgeting → risk-planning → phased-pilot → training → ongoing-support sequence, with a "technology champion" and help-desk access recommended for sustaining adoption [Tabernacle.app].

### 4. Does Hallow's/YouVersion's institutional model have a leader-first analog?

No faith-specific precedent was found. SmartGroups, the closest direct comparable, discloses no growth-tactics case study or partnership data on its own About page beyond a leader-empathy founder narrative and its free-tier/pilot structure [SmartGroups About]. The leader-first alternative is instead supported only by adjacent, non-faith analogs: EdTech's well-documented "teacher-first" bottom-up adoption pattern, in which the teacher (or here, the small-group leader) becomes a free proxy sales channel who can later "band together" with peers to trigger a top-down institutional deal — explicitly framed by its source as applicable to classroom-facing products specifically, not admin-facing tools [Reach Capital]. This top-down-vs-bottom-up framing is a named, recognized dichotomy in edtech sales strategy more broadly [EdSurge]. The mechanism is reinforced by agile-ed's "champion enablement kit" concept, in which the champion — not a vendor sales team — presents to the institutional decision body [Agile Education Marketing]. This is a structural analog worth adapting, not a faith-context precedent (see Risks).

### 5. Structural analogs outside the faith-app space

Beyond EdTech, two further non-faith analogs corroborate a leader/champion-first go-to-market motion: general community-led-growth literature recommends seeding a product with its most engaged, articulate, generous early users first, since the first 20-50 participants set behavioral norms for everyone who follows, with champions later converting into referral sources via peer recommendations [Salesfully]. Notion's Campus Leaders/Ambassador Program is a documented, named, non-faith example of exactly this shape applied at the campus/student level — leaders host events, build resources, give product feedback, and educate peers, incentivized with free product access and exclusive resources rather than cash [Notion Ambassador Program]. Both are plausible transferable patterns for FellowScript but require adaptation from a for-profit SaaS/consumer context into a faith setting.

### 6. Campus-ministry tactics beyond "relational beats digital"

EveryCampus's own strategy and launch-guide pages (primary sources) confirm rather than merely corroborate the relational emphasis already known from the prior task: launch guidance foregrounds small-group Bible studies, mentorship, prayer walks, and ministry coaching over digital growth mechanics [EveryCampus Strategy; EveryCampus Launch Guide]. However, neither page publicly documents chapter-by-chapter rollout mechanics, staffing ratios, or team-size requirements — deeper operational detail sits behind a gated community platform this round could not access. A case study of EveryCampus's app (built by agency Agathon) confirms the model reached all 4,234 US college campuses by May 2020, but attributes this to a coalition of established ministry organizations (Cru, InterVarsity, etc.), not a small-team playbook [Agathon/EveryCampus case study]. For what a small-team version might look like, the best available material is non-faith: Future.com's startup-ambassador-program guide recommends starting with a single "campus lead" sourced via personal referral, who then recruits and manages other reps — a hierarchical delegation model that scales outreach without a large staff — using non-financial incentives (product affinity, resume value, exclusive access) and timing launches to the academic calendar/high-traffic events like welcome week. The same source cautions that launching an ambassador program before achieving product-market fit/retention risks burning the audience [Future.com Ambassador Guide]. Its case examples (Tinder, Bumble, Discord, Grubhub, Sidechat) are for-profit consumer apps, so transfer to campus ministry is an extrapolation.

### 7. A prioritized, resource-realistic playbook given FellowScript's small-team constraint

Synthesizing the above into effort-vs-plausible-yield tiers:

- **Low-effort, precedent-backed (do now):** free-tier/pilot offers as foot-in-the-door for small-group leaders [Text In Church; SmartGroups; Agile Education Marketing]; hands-on group onboarding at first meeting [ChurchTechToday]; positioning the app as the group's central hub with periodic leader check-ins for retention [ChurchTechToday].
- **Moderate-effort, hypothesis-adapted-from-adjacent-domain (pilot small, validate before scaling):** identifying and enabling a small number of high-engagement early-adopter leaders as champions who can pull peers in, per the community-led-growth and Notion Ambassador patterns [Salesfully; Notion Ambassador Program]; role-differentiated pitch framing for pastors by decision-style [Text In Church].
- **High-effort, institution-scale, lower near-term plausible yield given team size:** formal diocese/parish-office partnerships modeled on Hallow/YouVersion, given the compliance/liability evaluation burden documented for diocesan gatekeepers [Diocese policy sources; Tabernacle.app institutional rollout sequence] and the small-team capacity constraint carried forward from the prior task.
- **Campus-ministry coalitions specifically:** the evidence supports deferring rather than pursuing now. EveryCampus's own materials confirm the model operates as a 40+-organization coalition [EveryCampus Strategy], its precedent-scale success was coalition-driven [Agathon/EveryCampus case study], and no faith-specific small-team version is documented. A single-campus-lead delegation model [Future.com Ambassador Guide] is a plausible smaller-scale hypothesis worth testing later, but only after — not instead of — validating lower-effort small-group-leader-direct tactics, consistent with the prior task's already-validated lower-effort channels (ASO, referral mechanics).

### 8. Retention/relationship-building once a gatekeeper says yes

Recurring, corroborated retention tactics: periodic leader check-ins and troubleshooting support through the season, framed as ongoing partnership rather than a one-time sale [ChurchTechToday]; a designated "technology champion" plus help-desk access to sustain institutional-scale adoption post-rollout [Tabernacle.app]; and, at the earliest-adopter stage, converting champions into referral sources via peer-network recommendations and user-generated content such as tutorials or case studies [Salesfully]. These are consistent across faith-specific and general-B2B sources, giving this step comparatively strong corroboration relative to the other steps.

## Risks

- **No faith-specific leader-first precedent for the core open question.** The central question — whether a leader-first partnership shape is a documented alternative to Hallow's/YouVersion's institution-first model — is answered only via non-faith analogs (EdTech, community-led growth, Notion). These are well-corroborated in their own domains but their transfer to a faith-app context is an extrapolation, not a proven pattern, and should be treated as a hypothesis per the plan's standing requirement.
- **Asymmetric role coverage.** Small-group-leader material is tactical and solid; pastor material is present but one dedicated source explicitly fails to differentiate pastors from generic leadership (evidence the differentiated literature is thin); ministry-coordinator material is the thinnest (role definition only, no pitch guidance); diocese/parish-administrator material is real but narrow, skews toward compliance/liability rather than persuasion angles, and originates from UK Anglican/GDPR-context documents whose specific legal regime may not transfer to a US-inflected target audience.
- **Commercial interest in several sources.** Reach Capital (VC content marketing), Agile Education Marketing (edtech marketing agency), Text In Church (vendor help center), Tabernacle.app (vendor blog), Salesfully (B2B marketing agency), and the Notion Ambassador writeup (marketing-agency analysis, though of a well-documented third-party program) all have some commercial motive in the claims they publish. None appear to be selling the exact tactic FellowScript would use, which mitigates but does not eliminate the concern.
- **EveryCampus operational detail is gated.** The deeper chapter-by-chapter rollout mechanics, staffing ratios, and any small-team-scale version of the EveryCampus model sit behind a community platform this round could not access — the "relational beats digital" caution from the prior task is reconfirmed, but the "how, concretely, with a small team" question remains only partially answered.
- **No literal outreach scripts found.** No source supplied a cold-outreach email/DM script template for pastors or small-group leaders — a plan-requested content-format that was not fulfilled by this sourcing round.
- **SmartGroups (closest comparable) discloses no growth data.** This means the single most directly relevant comparable cannot corroborate or falsify any tactic proposed here; all comparisons to it are structural (tiering, pilot offer) rather than outcome-based.

## Open Questions

- Is there any faith-specific (not adjacent-domain) precedent for a leader-first, non-institutional partnership motion that a deeper or differently-targeted search round might surface — e.g., smaller devotional/Bible-study apps below Hallow/YouVersion's scale that grew leader-by-leader?
- What would the gated EveryCampus "Start Something New" community platform reveal about small-team feasibility, and is it worth attempting access (e.g., via direct outreach to EveryCampus) in a later stage?
- How well do the EdTech teacher-champion and Notion Campus Leaders analogs actually hold up when adapted to a faith context, where trust/doctrinal-safety concerns (flagged in step 3) may create adoption friction that classroom or general-SaaS champions don't face?
- Given the compliance-heavy, UK-context nature of the diocese/parish-administrator sources, does a US-context diocese/parish-administrator gatekeeper face materially different adoption criteria (e.g., state privacy law, denominational polity differences between Catholic dioceses and Protestant parish structures) that would change the pitch angle for that specific role?
- Is a coordinator-specific pitch angle discoverable with more targeted searching, or is this role's absence from the literature itself informative (i.e., coordinators may not be an independent decision-maker but rather an implementer who acts on a pastor's or diocese's decision)?
- Should the critiquing stage specifically stress-test whether the "defer campus-ministry coalitions" recommendation in step 7 holds up against counter-evidence, given it rests on an absence of a small-team precedent rather than a documented failure of small-team campus-ministry attempts?
