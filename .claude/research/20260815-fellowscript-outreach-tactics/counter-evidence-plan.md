# Counter-Evidence Plan: FellowScript Gatekeeper Outreach Tactics

## Target claims

Selected because they are load-bearing for the brief's central recommendations, rest on thin or absence-based corroboration, or are carried forward with more confidence than this round's sourcing independently verified. The brief's own Risks and Open Questions sections flag several of these as needing exactly this kind of stress-test.

1. **"No faith-specific leader-first precedent was found."** (Section 4, restated in Risks as "The central question ... is answered only via non-faith analogs.") — This is the answer to the task's core open question. It is an absence-of-evidence claim resting on one sourcing round; the brief's own Open Questions ask whether "a deeper or differently-targeted search round might surface" a faith-specific precedent.

2. **"[Campus-ministry coalitions specifically:] the evidence supports deferring rather than pursuing now."** (Section 7) — The brief's own Open Questions explicitly asks the critiquing stage to "stress-test whether the 'defer campus-ministry coalitions' recommendation ... holds up against counter-evidence, given it rests on an absence of a small-team precedent rather than a documented failure of small-team campus-ministry attempts." This recommendation is directly actionable for FellowScript, making it high-stakes to get right.

3. **"Evangelical campus-outreach practitioners caution that relational/on-the-ground tactics outperform broad digital advertising specifically in the campus-ministry sub-audience"** (Section 1, carried forward from the prior task as settled background and used again in Section 6/7 as a premise for the deferral recommendation) — This is a comparative "X outperforms Y" claim. Per `sources.json`, its original corroboration was practitioner caution/commentary (TheGospelCoalition, corroborated by Reliant Creative), not a comparative outcome study. This round's EveryCampus primary sources "confirm" it only by showing the org *foregrounds* relational tactics in its own materials — foregrounding is not the same as documented comparative outperformance. The claim is stated with more confidence (a settled fact restated across two tasks) than either round's sourcing actually establishes.

4. **The EdTech teacher-first / Notion Campus Leaders "leader-first go-to-market" pattern is treated as "a structural analog worth adapting"** for a faith-app context (Sections 4-5), despite the brief's own Open Questions flagging that "trust/doctrinal-safety concerns ... may create adoption friction that classroom or general-SaaS champions don't face." The brief adopts the analog as the primary answer to the core question while only naming, not testing, the reason it might not transfer.

5. **Diocese/parish-administrator material "skews toward compliance and liability rather than persuasion"** and both compliance sources are "UK Anglican/GDPR documents, a regime-mismatch caveat for a likely US-inflected audience" (Section 3, Risks) — presented as a coverage gap/limitation rather than tested against the possibility that US-context, persuasion-oriented diocese/parish-technology-adoption material exists and would change the pitch angle for this gatekeeper role.

## Search angles

### 1. Faith-specific leader-first precedent
Genuine counter-evidence would be a documented case (ideally with some growth/adoption detail, not just a founder narrative) of a faith-specific product — Bible study, devotional, or small-group tool — that grew primarily leader-by-leader rather than through formal institutional/diocese partnerships. Look at:
- YouVersion/Bible App's own early growth history (pre-institutional-partnership era) — LifeChurch/Bobby Gruenewald accounts of organic, pastor-word-of-mouth-driven adoption before the parish-partnership model matured.
- RightNow Media — explicitly a small-group video curriculum product sold at the leader/church level; look for its own go-to-market or sales-motion descriptions (company blog, founder interviews, case studies), not just its marketing copy.
- Smaller/newer entrants (Dwell, Alabaster, Echo, GodTools, theBible.org tools, D-Group) — search for founder interviews, "how we grew" retrospectives, or startup-press coverage describing a leader-first motion specifically.
- Also actively look for a documented *failure* of a faith-app leader-first attempt — a genuine negative case is still counter-evidence to "no precedent found" (it demonstrates the pattern was attempted and is discoverable, even if it didn't work), and separately informs the playbook's risk assessment.
- Do not accept another marketing-agency's abstract commentary about "leader-first works" as counter-evidence here — it must be a concrete faith-specific case, or explicit documentation that none exists after a genuinely different search strategy (different query terms, founder/press sources instead of vendor/agency sources).

### 2. "Defer campus-ministry coalitions"
Genuine counter-evidence would be a documented case of a small team (not a 40+-org coalition) running a campus-ministry outreach or digital-tool-adoption effort with plausible success — or evidence that campus ministries below EveryCampus's scale have adopted small-group/Bible-study tools without needing a coalition structure. Look at:
- Single-campus or single-organization campus ministry tech adoption stories (individual Cru/InterVarsity/RUF chapter blogs or newsletters describing a small-team tool rollout, not the national coalition level).
- Campus-ministry-adjacent startups smaller than EveryCampus (e.g., single-campus prayer/Bible apps, RUF or Navigators chapter-level tools) with any documented team size or growth detail.
- Direct evidence bearing on whether the *deferral* itself is risky — e.g., material suggesting campus ministry networks are unusually receptive to small, informal digital tools introduced by a single trusted staffer (which would argue for pursuing now, not deferring).
- Avoid sources that just repeat "campus ministry is relational" as a truism without addressing team size/scale at all — that doesn't move the deferral question either way.

### 3. "Relational beats digital" in campus ministry — comparative strength check
Genuine counter-evidence would be data-oriented (even survey/comparative, not just practitioner opinion) evidence that digital outreach or digital tools have worked well in a campus-ministry or youth/young-adult faith context. Look at:
- Barna Group research on Gen Z/young-adult digital faith engagement (Barna publishes youth/faith-tech studies regularly and is a more empirical source than the practitioner blogs already cited).
- Documented digital-first campus ministry successes — e.g., YouVersion's own campus-specific programs, JESUS Film Project or Cru digital evangelism reporting any campus-specific metrics, or campus-ministry social-media case studies with engagement/conversion numbers.
- Counter-evidence here could also come from a source that complicates the binary — e.g., showing relational tactics work better for *initial* engagement but digital tools work well for *sustaining* or *scaling* an already-relational relationship (this would refine rather than overturn the claim, which is still a valid and reportable outcome).
- Explicitly distinguish "practitioner opinion/caution" (what's already cited) from "comparative outcome evidence" (what's being sought) — restating the same TheGospelCoalition-style commentary from a different outlet does not count as counter-evidence.

### 4. EdTech/Notion leader-first analog transferring to a trust/doctrinal-safety context
Genuine counter-evidence would be a case where a bottom-up, individual-champion adoption motion failed or stalled specifically because of a trust, compliance, safety, or gatekeeping barrier that classroom/general-SaaS contexts don't usually have — the closer to a faith, health, or child-safety context, the more relevant. Look at:
- EdTech or health-tech products with FERPA/HIPAA-like compliance requirements where teacher/clinician-level bottom-up adoption stalled at the institutional-approval stage despite grassroots enthusiasm (this would be structurally close to a doctrinal-safety gate).
- Church-technology-adoption retrospectives (ChurchTechToday, Church Executive, or similar trade press already used in this research) specifically describing a leader-championed app or tool that was blocked, delayed, or rolled back by pastoral/doctrinal review after grassroots adoption began.
- Any direct commentary (from the church-technology or faith-app press already surfaced in this research, e.g. Concordia Technology, Tabernacle.app, ChurchTechToday) about doctrinal-safety or theological-vetting requirements specifically gating bottom-up tool adoption in a church.

### 5. Diocese/parish persuasion material, US context
Genuine counter-evidence would be US-context (not UK/Anglican) diocese- or parish-office-facing material that addresses *persuasion* (why to adopt) rather than only compliance (how to stay compliant once adopted). Look at:
- USCCB (US Conference of Catholic Bishops) communications-office resources or guidance on parish technology/app adoption.
- US diocese-specific technology or communications-office case studies (search diocesan communications-office blogs, Catholic press like America Magazine, National Catholic Reporter, or OSV/Our Sunday Visitor for parish-technology-adoption coverage).
- Existing US parish-app/parish-management vendors (e.g., Diocesan.com/myParish app, Flocknote, ParishSoft) — their own sales/pitch material aimed at diocese-level decision-makers would show what a persuasion (not just compliance) angle looks like for this exact audience, even though vendor-authored.
- If no US-context persuasion-oriented material surfaces after genuinely different search terms, that absence should be reported as informative (the gap may itself indicate dioceses are approached relationally/institutionally rather than via public-facing persuasion content), not treated as a failed search.

## Constraints

Inherited from `research-plan.md`:
- Source types: mix of faith-based-app industry material, general B2B/B2G "champion/gatekeeper" sales and partnership literature (including EdTech-analog sources), ministry/church-marketing agency material, and campus-ministry-specific guidance. Academic/empirical sourcing (e.g., Barna) is welcome and especially valuable for angle 3, which needs comparative rather than anecdotal evidence.
- Time period: prioritize last 3-5 years; up to ~5 years acceptable given slow-moving church-technology-adoption norms.
- Must explicitly flag, for any counter-evidence found, whether it's a genuine faith-specific/small-team precedent or another adjacent-domain analog being proposed as a further hypothesis.
- Never re-cite the four debunked/flagged figures from the prior task: the 83% referral-trust stat, the 30% Catholic-org partnership increase, the February 2024 Hallow spike as calendar-timing-alone proof, and the blanket "authenticity beats production" claim.

Counter-evidence-specific:
- Do not count a source as counter-evidence if it merely restates the original claim from a different outlet (e.g., another practitioner blog repeating "relational beats digital" without new data) — that is corroboration, not challenge, and should not be reported as if it moves the needle.
- Prefer primary or empirical sources (founder accounts, case studies with concrete detail, survey/comparative research) over secondhand commentary, mirroring the standard already applied in `sources.json`.
- Continue applying the same commercial-interest scrutiny used in sourcing: vendor and agency sources are usable but their claims should be weighed accordingly, especially for angle 5 where the most available US-context material will likely be vendor pitch pages.
- Where a genuine, good-faith counter-evidence search turns up nothing (a real possibility for angle 1 and angle 5 given how thin the underlying literature already is), report the absence explicitly and describe what was searched, rather than stretching a weak or tangential source to fill the gap.

## Success criteria

A good-faith counter-evidence effort for this topic:
- For each of the five target claims, runs search queries that are genuinely different in framing from the ones that produced the original brief's sources (not just negations of the same keywords) — e.g., founder-retrospective and press-coverage searches for angle 1, empirical/survey-research searches for angle 3, US-diocese-specific vendor and press searches for angle 5.
- Produces, per claim, one of three honest outcomes: (a) real disconfirming or complicating evidence found, (b) the original claim holds up because a genuine search found nothing to challenge it, or (c) the evidence is mixed/refines rather than overturns the claim (as anticipated for angle 3). All three are valid, reportable results — the goal is a genuine test, not manufactured disagreement.
- Keeps the highest scrutiny on claim 2 (defer campus-ministry coalitions) and claim 1 (no faith-specific leader-first precedent), since these are the most directly actionable for FellowScript and the brief itself flagged them as most in need of stress-testing.
- Does not treat the UK-vs-US regulatory distinction (claim 5) as resolved by finding *any* US material — it should specifically assess whether persuasion angle, not just compliance framing, differs by region.
- Feeds cleanly into the critique stage: each claim should end this step with a clear determination of whether it should be softened, retained as-is, or flagged as now actively contested in `critique.md`.
