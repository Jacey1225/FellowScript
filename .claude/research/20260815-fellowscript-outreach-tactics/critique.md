# Critique: FellowScript Gatekeeper Outreach Tactics

This critique puts each of the five target claims from `brief.md` side by side with its counter-claim from `counter-claims.json` and stress-tests both directions rather than defaulting to the original brief.

## Per-claim verdicts

### 1. "No faith-specific leader-first precedent exists" (brief, Section 4 / Risks)

- **Original**: The leader-first alternative to Hallow's/YouVersion's institution-first model is supported only by non-faith analogs (EdTech, community-led growth, Notion) — no faith-specific precedent was found.
- **Counter**: YouVersion's own 2008–2012 growth was organic/individual/viral (83K opening-weekend installs; ~1M installs in 11 days via "Share the Bible Day"), predating its institutional partnerships — a faith-specific counterexample to "institution-first is the only documented faith-app pattern."
- **Verdict: original survives, and is actually sharpened.** The counter-evidence author is honest that this is "user-first," not "leader-first" — and on inspection the gap is more structural than the counter-claim's hedging suggests, not just a matter of degree. YouVersion is a free, individually-downloadable product with no group-gatekeeper dependency at all; its viral growth required no leader or champion to unlock access for anyone else. FellowScript is structurally gated — pricing is per-group, so a leader/gatekeeper is a precondition for any user to get in, in a way that has no analog in YouVersion's early growth story. That makes YouVersion's precedent weaker, not stronger, as an answer to the specific question the brief needs answered (does a leader-as-champion motion have faith-specific precedent?) — it's not just "a different flavor of the same thing," it's evidence for a different problem shape entirely. A genuinely adversarial search still turned up nothing that puts a leader/champion in YouVersion's early growth account. The original claim holds.

### 2. "Defer campus-ministry coalitions" (brief, Section 7)

- **Original**: Given EveryCampus's precedent-scale success was coalition-driven and no small-team faith-specific version is documented, defer coalition investment in favor of validating lower-effort channels first.
- **Counter**: A genuinely different search strategy (chapter-level blogs, staffer-adoption, RUF/InterVarsity/Cru-specific searches) found no small-team success case and no small-team failure case either — the absence holds up. An independent, non-vendor source (First Things) additionally reports that campus ministries with dedicated budget/staff/facilities measurably outperform under-resourced student-org-style ministries, reinforcing rather than undermining the infrastructure-scale argument behind the deferral.
- **Verdict: original survives and gains confidence, but the underlying epistemic status should stay honest.** This is the best-supported claim in the set: the counter-evidence agent tried genuinely to falsify it and instead found corroboration from an independent source with no stake in the recommendation. That said, the deferral recommendation still rests on an absence of a documented small-team precedent, not a documented small-team failure — First Things' finding (resourced ministries outperform under-resourced ones) is about ministry operations broadly, not about a small team specifically attempting a coalition-style digital-tool push and failing. The brief's own confidence in "defer" should rise, but it should still be stated as "unproven for small teams," not "disproven for small teams."

### 3. "Relational tactics outperform digital advertising in campus ministry" (carried forward from prior task, reconfirmed in brief Section 6)

- **Original**: Evangelical campus-outreach practitioners caution that relational/on-the-ground tactics outperform broad digital advertising in the campus-ministry sub-audience.
- **Counter**: No direct comparative outcome study exists. The best empirical source (Barna, Gen-Z-wide not campus-ministry-specific) shows 54% of Gen Z prefer in-person relationships but explicitly recommends a complementary framing (digital enhancing, not competing with, face-to-face) rather than a strict "relational wins" binary. A stats aggregator (Gitnux) was found and correctly rejected as unreliable.
- **Verdict: original claim does not fully hold as stated — it is overstated and should be softened.** "Outperform" is a comparative-effectiveness claim that no source, on either side, actually measured. The strongest available empirical data point (Barna) contradicts the binary framing specifically, not just adding nuance to it: Barna's own recommended strategy is complementary use, which is a different claim than "relational outperforms digital." This is a case where the weaker side (the counter-evidence) is actually right to push back — the original claim should be downgraded from "outperforms" to "relational tactics matter more for initial trust-building; digital and relational are complementary rather than competing." This changes the practical implication for FellowScript's playbook: digital channels for campus ministry aren't necessarily to be minimized, just sequenced after/alongside relational trust-building rather than treated as an inferior substitute.

### 4. "EdTech teacher-first pattern is a structural analog worth adapting" (brief Section 4, Risks)

- **Original**: The EdTech leader-first pattern is treated as a structural analog worth adapting, with trust/doctrinal-safety concerns named as a caveat but not tested.
- **Counter**: EdTech's own literature documents a "pilot trap" — champion-dependent results that are tied to one teacher's skill/context and don't transfer institution-wide — plus a FERPA compliance trigger the moment a teacher-champion uses the tool with students, forcing a compliance/IT review gate before scaling, structurally similar to a doctrinal-safety review gate FellowScript might hit.
- **Verdict: this is genuine, credible counter-evidence that survives scrutiny, and it should change the brief, not just append to it.** The source (SchoolAI) has a commercial interest (selling adoption solutions), which cuts both ways — it has an incentive to describe adoption as failure-prone (to sell the fix), but that incentive doesn't change the underlying documented mechanism (FERPA triggers on first institutional use), which is a matter of law, not opinion, and is independently plausible. This is exactly the kind of test the brief's Risks section called for but didn't perform. Verdict: the analog itself is not undermined as a growth *mechanism*, but its assumed smoothness is — champion-first adoption in the source domain has a known failure mode at the exact institutional-gatekeeping seam (compliance/doctrinal review) that the brief already flagged as an open risk for FellowScript. This is confirmation of a risk, not refutation of the tactic, but it means "structural analog worth adapting" should carry a stronger caveat than the brief's current one-line mention.

### 5. "Diocese material skews toward compliance, and both compliance sources are UK Anglican/GDPR" (brief Section 3, Risks)

- **Original**: Available diocese/parish-administrator material skews toward compliance and liability rather than persuasion; both compliance sources are UK Anglican/GDPR documents, a regime-mismatch caveat for a likely US audience.
- **Counter**: A US vendor (Flocknote) maintains a diocese-facing marketing page using persuasion/benefit language (master data list, bishop communication channel, ready-made campaign templates) rather than compliance framing — contradicting the "compliance-only, UK-only" framing. However, the live page returned an HTTP 403; the assessment relies on a search-indexed snippet, not a full read, and no independent/denominational-body persuasion material was found to corroborate it.
- **Verdict: the counter-claim only partially holds, and the original claim survives with a narrower scope.** The specific compliance sources cited in the brief genuinely are UK/Anglican/GDPR documents — that fact is not contradicted. What the counter-evidence actually shows is that the *broader universe* of diocese-facing material isn't compliance-only; US persuasion material exists, but only from a self-interested vendor, and even that evidence is weakly verified (blocked fetch, snippet-only). Given the verification weakness, this should be treated as suggestive rather than established. The original claim's core caveat (the *specific documents cited* are UK-context, and independent/denominational persuasion material for a US audience remains undiscovered) still stands.

## Claims that hold

- **No faith-specific leader-first precedent exists** (original) — survives, and the counter-evidence's own structural gap (user-first ≠ leader-first, no-gatekeeper ≠ gatekeeper-dependent) makes the original finding more defensible than before the critique, not less.
- **Defer campus-ministry coalitions for now** (original) — survives with increased confidence; independent corroboration was found where counter-evidence was sought.
- **EdTech scaling risk is real and understated in the brief's Risks section** (counter) — this counter-claim holds up: champion-dependent adoption has a documented failure mode at the institutional-gatekeeping seam, which is directly relevant to FellowScript's doctrinal-safety concern.
- **UK-context compliance sources genuinely are UK-context** (original, narrow reading) — survives; the counter-evidence complicates the broader picture but doesn't overturn the specific sourcing caveat.

## Claims that don't hold

- **"Relational tactics outperform digital advertising" (as a strict comparative/outperformance claim)** — does not survive as stated. No source on either side measured comparative outperformance; the best empirical evidence (Barna) explicitly recommends a complementary framing instead of a binary one. This claim should be downgraded to "relational tactics likely matter more for initial trust-building; digital and relational are complementary rather than competing" — the sharper original framing was an overstatement carried forward from the prior task without being tested until now.
- **"Diocese material is compliance-only" (as an unqualified claim about the full landscape, not just the cited sources)** — does not survive in its broadest reading. Persuasion-oriented, US-context material does exist in the wider landscape (via vendors), even though the brief's own cited sources are compliance-focused and UK-context. The brief's phrasing should be read narrowly (about its own citations) rather than as a claim that no persuasion-oriented US material exists anywhere.

## Confidence adjustments

- **Claim 1 (no faith-specific leader-first precedent)**: brief's implicit confidence should rise slightly. A genuinely adversarial search found only a structurally weaker analog (user-first, gatekeeper-free), which is closer to confirming than refuting the absence.
- **Claim 2 (defer campus-ministry coalitions)**: confidence should rise meaningfully — from "reasoned inference from absence of evidence" to "reasoned inference from absence of evidence, tested and reinforced by independent reporting." Still should not be treated as "proven small teams fail," only as "unproven that small teams succeed, and directionally consistent with an infrastructure/scale-advantage account."
- **Claim 3 (relational outperforms digital)**: confidence should fall and the claim itself should be reworded. Carry the softened "complementary, sequenced" framing forward into the conclusion stage rather than the original "outperform" framing.
- **Claim 4 (EdTech analog transfers cleanly)**: confidence in the analog's *core mechanism* (champion-led bottom-up adoption) is roughly unchanged, but confidence that it transfers *smoothly, without institutional friction* should fall. The brief's Risks section should treat the doctrinal-safety concern as a documented pattern-of-friction risk (per the source-domain's own FERPA-trigger precedent), not merely a speculative caveat.
- **Claim 5 (diocese material is compliance/UK-only)**: confidence in the narrow claim (about the brief's own two cited sources) is unchanged. Confidence in any broader implicit claim that no US/persuasion material exists anywhere should fall, but only slightly, given the counter-evidence's own verification weakness (blocked page, snippet-only, single vendor source, no independent corroboration).

## Unresolved

- Whether a faith-specific *leader-first* (not merely user-first) precedent exists remains genuinely open — this round's search (this task) and the counter-evidence round both came up empty, but neither constitutes an exhaustive search of the smaller/niche devotional-app space the brief itself flagged as an open question.
- Whether a small team can run a viable campus-ministry outreach motion below coalition scale remains empirically untested in either direction — absence of both success and failure cases persists after two independently-framed search efforts.
- The true shape of the US-context diocese/parish-administrator gatekeeper's persuasion criteria remains unresolved — the one data point found (Flocknote) is vendor-sourced, weakly verified, and uncorroborated by any independent or denominational body.

## Overall assessment

Three of five claims held up well and, in two cases, gained confidence under scrutiny (no faith-specific leader-first precedent; defer campus-ministry coalitions). One claim was genuinely weakened and should be reworded before it reaches the conclusion stage (relational-outperforms-digital, now complementary/sequenced). One claim from the counter-evidence side held up as a legitimate, source-independent complication that strengthens the brief's existing risk framing rather than replacing it (EdTech adoption's compliance-trigger failure mode). The diocese/compliance claim survives narrowly but should not be read as an unqualified claim about the whole US landscape. No claim was found to be untraceable to its cited source, and the counter-evidence side was substantive enough on every target claim to critique meaningfully — this does not warrant a bounce.
