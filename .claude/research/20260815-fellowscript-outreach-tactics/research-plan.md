- The prior brief found pastor/leader outreach and "best-of" list placement (e.g., TheLeadPastor) is a real, targetable discovery channel, and that campus-ministry coalition apps (EveryCampus, 100+ campus ministries) are a live precedented model — but evangelical campus-outreach practitioners (TheGospelCoalition, corroborated by Reliant Creative) caution that relational/on-the-ground tactics outperform broad digital advertising specifically in the campus-ministry sub-audience.
- Hallow's institutional model rests on 2,500+ parish partnerships and ~9% QR-code-attributed growth (well-sourced) but the "30% user increase" figure from 2024 Catholic-org collaborations was debunked as untraceable — treat Hallow's model as a directional pattern, not a number to replicate.
- FellowScript is a small/indie team (confirmed via prior repo-signal analysis), which constrains how labor-intensive any outreach approach can be.

## Topic / Claim

What concrete outreach tactics, pitch angles, and relationship-building approaches should FellowScript use to reach and persuade church/parish/campus-ministry **gatekeepers** — pastors, small-group leaders, ministry coordinators, diocese/parish administrators — to adopt or partner with FellowScript, given it is a small-team, group-priced, group-study-centric Bible study app (not an institutional devotional app like Hallow, and not an individual-habit-loop app like YouVersion)?

## Scope

**In bounds:**
- How to identify, approach, and pitch small-group leaders and pastors directly (cold outreach, warm intros, in-person/relational tactics, email/DM scripts, demo/trial offers, free-tier-as-foot-in-the-door approaches) as a distinct motion from institutional (diocese/parish-office) partnership tactics.
- What pitch angles/value propositions resonate with each gatekeeper type (pastor vs. small-group leader vs. ministry coordinator vs. diocese/parish administrator) — these are not interchangeable audiences and the pitch likely needs to differ by role.
- Whether and how Hallow's/YouVersion's institutional parish-partnership playbook (formal diocese agreements, QR codes in bulletins, staff outreach teams) does or does not transfer to a smaller group-study product, and what a scaled-down or differently-shaped version might look like for a small team.
- Campus-ministry-specific outreach: what "relational, not digital" tactics concretely look like in practice for campus ministry (e.g., attending events, staff/leader relationships, chapter-by-chapter rollout, ambassador/rep models) and whether any of this is feasible for FellowScript's team size, or whether it should be deprioritized in favor of lower-effort channels already validated in the prior research (ASO, referral mechanics).
- Relationship-building/retention tactics once a gatekeeper has said yes (onboarding a group leader, keeping a pastor/coordinator engaged as a champion, turning early adopters into referral sources to other leaders) — this is in scope because "adopting or partnering" implies an ongoing relationship, not just a single conversion event.
- Precedents/case studies of other apps or products (faith-based or close analogs, e.g., B2B/B2G-style "sell to the leader who brings a group" models) successfully running leader-first (rather than institution-first) outreach motions, to test whether that shape is more appropriate for FellowScript than the diocese-style model.

**Out of bounds (already resolved in the prior task, reference rather than re-research):**
- FellowScript's mission, feature set, and differentiators (Reader, shared highlights, public notes, AI devotional check-ins).
- Brand/design identity and tone-of-voice questions.
- Channel selection at the macro level (referral vs. paid, ASO tactics) — except where a specific channel choice bears directly on gatekeeper outreach (e.g., referral mechanics *among leaders* is in bounds; general ASO is not).
- Comparable-app competitive positioning beyond what's needed to evaluate whether Hallow's/YouVersion's specific partnership tactics transfer.
- Monetization model structure/pricing-tier resolution (already flagged as unresolved upstream and not this task's job to settle) — treat the group-priced model as a given input, not a question to re-litigate.

**Narrowing note:** The request bundles four gatekeeper roles (pastors, small-group leaders, ministry coordinators, diocese/parish administrators) into one ask. This plan treats them as requiring potentially different tactics rather than assuming one pitch fits all, and organizes research steps accordingly. If sourcing turns up good material for some roles and little for others (plausible — diocese administrators are a narrower, more institutional audience than "small-group leaders" as a category), that asymmetry should be reported rather than papered over.

## Research Steps

1. What does the prior research already establish about pastor/leader outreach and campus-ministry coalition precedents (TheLeadPastor, faith.tools, EveryCampus, TheGospelCoalition, Reliant Creative sources), and what exactly did the caution about "relational tactics outperforming digital ones" say — what's the mechanism behind that claim, and what would relational-but-scalable-for-a-small-team look like in practice?
2. What concrete outreach tactics (channels, message formats, first-touch approaches) work for reaching individual pastors and small-group leaders directly, as distinct from an institutional/diocese sales motion — cold outreach scripts, warm-intro/referral chains, conference/event presence, content-as-outreach (e.g., free resources for leaders), free-tier/pilot-group offers?
3. What pitch angles and value propositions are documented as effective when pitching a technology/app adoption to a pastor specifically, vs. to a lay small-group leader, vs. to a paid ministry staff coordinator, vs. to a diocese/parish administrative office? Do these differ in emphasis (e.g., pastoral: doctrinal safety/trustworthiness; small-group leader: ease of facilitation; diocese: institutional liability/rollout logistics)?
4. Does Hallow's/YouVersion's institutional parish-partnership model (formal agreements, bulk QR-code distribution, dedicated outreach staff) have any documented smaller-scale or leader-first analog — i.e., has any faith-app or adjacent product succeeded by going leader-by-leader rather than institution-by-institution? What would a "leader-first" partnership shape look like concretely for FellowScript?
5. Are there close structural analogs outside the faith-app space — EdTech selling to individual teachers rather than school districts, or community/hobby apps selling to meetup/group organizers rather than platforms — that illuminate tactics for a "the group leader is the buyer/champion" go-to-market motion? Do those analogs' tactics plausibly transfer to a faith context?
6. What do practitioners say concretely about campus-ministry outreach tactics beyond the single "relational beats digital" caution — e.g., specifics on chapter/staff relationship-building, ambassador or campus-rep models, event presence, or seasonal (semester-start) timing — and is there any documented case of a small team running this successfully without a large outreach staff?
7. Given FellowScript's small-team capacity constraint (already established upstream), what does a prioritized, resource-realistic outreach playbook look like — which gatekeeper-outreach tactics are low-effort/high-plausible-yield vs. high-effort/institution-scale, and should campus-ministry coalitions be pursued now, deferred, or dropped relative to direct small-group-leader outreach?
8. Once a gatekeeper (pastor, leader, coordinator) says yes, what relationship-building/retention tactics turn them into an ongoing champion or referral source to other leaders — onboarding practices, keeping engagement, leader-to-leader referral chains?
