# Research Brief: Transforming Small/Local Models into Robust Coding Agents

## Summary

### 1. Tool-use / function-calling in small models

Small models (roughly sub-10B) increasingly rely on scaffolding tailored to their weaker instruction-following rather than on scale alone. Techniques include guided decoding and JSON-schema enforcement, validator-first execution loops, and targeted fine-tuning specifically for tool-calling (arxiv.org/abs/2510.03847). One preprint reports that targeted supervised fine-tuning of a 350M-parameter model (facebook/opt-350m) reached a 77.55% pass rate on ToolBench, exceeding much larger baselines (ChatGPT-CoT 26.00%, ToolLLaMA-DFS 30.18%, ToolLLaMA-CoT 16.27%) (arxiv.org/abs/2512.15943) — a striking result but from a single unreviewed preprint with no independent corroboration. The Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard (BFCL v4), the standard reference benchmark (methodology peer-reviewed at PMLR/ICML: proceedings.mlr.press/v267/patil25a.html; leaderboard at gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/leaderboard.html), shows small models such as xLAM-2-3b-fc-r (65.74% overall) and Qwen3-4B (62.04%) performing respectably on single-turn tasks, but multi-turn tool-use collapses sharply with scale — Qwen3-0.6B drops from 45.76% overall to just 1.38% on multi-turn settings. This is the clearest, most concretely evidenced finding on small-model tool-use limits: single-turn/schema-constrained calling is largely solved at small scale, multi-turn tool orchestration is not.

### 2. Fine-tuning, instruction-tuning, and distillation

Distillation from larger "teacher" models into small agentic students is well represented. A NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight paper (arxiv.org/abs/2505.17612) — the strongest-reliability source in this set, being peer-reviewed at a top venue — describes distilling agent reasoning and tool/code use into 0.5B–7B student models via first-thought-prefix prompting and self-consistent action generation, reporting that the 0.5B/1.5B/3B distilled models become competitive with next-tier-larger CoT-distilled models across eight reasoning tasks. MapCoder-Lite (arxiv.org/pdf/2509.17489) distills an entire multi-agent coding pipeline (planner/coder/debugger/retriever) into a single small LLM, evaluated on APPS, CodeContest, and xCodeEval. A practitioner-scale corroboration comes from a vendor engineering blog (distil labs), which benchmarked 12 small models across 8 tasks and reported a LoRA fine-tuning recipe (rank-32, lr 2e-5, 3 epochs) under which Qwen3-4B matched or exceeded a 120B+ teacher model after fine-tuning — treated here as supplementary, not primary, evidence given its vendor origin, but directionally consistent with the peer-reviewed distillation results.

### 3. Retrieval-augmentation as a capability compensator

RAG is used to offset small models' limited context and world knowledge in coding settings, though the sourced material does not explicitly target small models — applicability is inferred rather than author-stated. A repository-level RACG survey (arxiv.org/abs/2510.04905) taxonomizes retrieval substrates and control regimes and flags an open question of where RACG stops being necessary versus long-context alone. A companion preprint, Meta-RAG (arxiv.org/abs/2508.02611), reports concrete results on SWE-bench Lite: LLM-generated codebase summaries achieve a 79.8% average codebase-size reduction while reaching 84.67% file-level and 53.0% function-level bug-localization accuracy, described as state-of-the-art. Notably, this connects to research step 8: a separate process-verification paper (below) finds RAG interventions consistently and measurably help small-model performance (Cohen's d 0.23–0.93), making RAG one of the better-evidenced compensators in this survey even though the RAG-specific papers themselves don't isolate small models.

### 4. Multi-agent / pipeline orchestration of small models

Two architectures dominate the evidence here. ProST (arxiv.org/pdf/2509.04508) trains multiple small models via progressive sub-task specialization so that the ensemble collectively matches or exceeds both a single large model and other multi-agent baselines, evaluated on AppWorld and SWE-Bench. MapCoder-Lite (above) represents the inverse move — collapsing a multi-agent pipeline into one small model via distillation rather than running multiple small models live. Together these suggest two competing design philosophies (live multi-small-model orchestration vs. distilling multi-agent behavior into a single model) without a source directly comparing the two approaches head-to-head.

### 5. Prompting / reasoning scaffolds for small models

This is one of the best-evidenced and most consequential categories, containing a genuine tension. SWRV (aclanthology.org/2025.xllm-1.29.pdf, ACL workshop, peer-reviewed) and CoCoS (arxiv.org/html/2505.23060, corroborated by an independent ACL Findings EMNLP 2025 listing at aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.127.pdf) both report gains from self-verification/self-correction training: CoCoS uses RL-based multi-turn self-correction on 1B-scale coding models (Qwen2.5-1.5B, Llama-3.2-1B, DeepSeek-Coder-1.3B), reporting 35.8% improvement on MBPP and 27.7% on HumanEval, but explicitly notes its evaluation is restricted to 1B-scale models with training capped at two turns due to RL cost — an unresolved scalability question. Contradicting the general assumption that self-verification scaffolds straightforwardly help, a strong empirical paper (arxiv.org/html/2601.00513, 10,734 reasoning traces across 3 models × 3 datasets, F1 0.86 for its proposed verification classifier) finds that 50–69% of small models' *correct* answers involve flawed reasoning ("right for wrong reasons," varying by model: Mistral-7B 50.2%, Llama-3-8B 55.2%, Qwen-2.5-7B 69.3%), and that self-critique/verification-prompt interventions consistently *harm* small-model performance (d −0.14 to −0.33) via "pseudo-reflection," while RAG interventions consistently help. This paper explicitly caveats its own generalizability: findings rest on oracle (perfect) RAG and only three tested 7–9B models.

### 6. Benchmarks and the small-vs-large gap

A rich, well-corroborated benchmark landscape was surfaced: BFCL (tool-use, described above), ToolBench, and several SWE-bench variants. SWE-bench Verified remains a headline number — Devstral Small 1.1 (24B) reportedly reaches 53.6% without test-time scaling, described by its vendor as state-of-the-art among open code-agent models (mistral.ai/news/devstral-2507/), up from the original Devstral Small's 46.8% (huggingface.co/mistralai/Devstral-Small-2505) — a trajectory independently restated (not independently measured) by a third-party blog (adam.holter.com). But harder, newer benchmarks complicate the optimistic small-model narrative: SWE-Bench Pro (arxiv.org/pdf/2509.16941) finds the best models of *any* size solve under 20% of its harder commercial task set, and SWE-EVO (arxiv.org/html/2512.18470v5) shows the best model solving only 25% of long-horizon software-evolution tasks versus 72.80% on standard SWE-Bench Verified by a comparable-generation model — indicating that as task horizon and realism increase, capability gaps grow sharply *regardless of model tier*, not just for small models. SWE-Compass (arxiv.org/pdf/2511.05459) is a unified evaluation framework spanning many model families but its granular small-vs-large breakdown was not extractable from available material — a partial-coverage source.

### 7. Named systems and notable projects

The NVIDIA position paper (arxiv.org/abs/2506.02153) is the most-cited anchor defining this space, arguing sub-10B SLMs on consumer hardware can match or beat larger models on many agentic subtasks at 10–30x lower cost — a claim treated as a strong industry-lab position rather than independently verified fact. Devstral (Mistral AI, 24B) is the most concretely evidenced named model, corroborated across three independent artifacts (vendor announcement, Hugging Face model card, third-party blog), explicitly designed for compatibility with multiple agentic scaffolds (OpenHands, XML function-calling) rather than one fixed harness, and reported to outperform far larger models (e.g., DeepSeek-V3-0324, Qwen3 232B-A22B) under the same OpenHands scaffold — supporting a "scaffolding matters as much as raw scale" thread. On the open-source tooling side, Aider, OpenCode, and Continue are catalogued as local-model-capable CLI coding agents across two independent vendor comparison blogs (morphllm.com, pinggy.io) — both supplementary sources, not technical evidence in themselves, but consistent with each other on the same named-projects list.

### 8. Consensus / disagreement on which technique matters most

No single source directly compares technique categories head-to-head; the picture below is assembled by triangulation across sources, as flagged in the gathering agent's own gap notes. NVIDIA's position paper argues SLMs plus proper scaffolding/orchestration are already sufficient or superior for a majority of agentic subtasks (arxiv.org/abs/2506.02153), independently corroborated in framing by a second survey (arxiv.org/abs/2510.03847). Devstral's benchmark trajectory supports scaffolding/fine-tuning combined mattering more than raw parameter count. But this optimism is complicated by two harder-evidence threads: (a) SWE-bench Pro and SWE-EVO show that on realistic, long-horizon tasks even the best models of any size are far from solved, suggesting the small-vs-large "gap" question is somewhat secondary to a harder underlying agentic-competence ceiling; and (b) the process-verification paper's quantified finding that self-critique/verification prompting actively *harms* small-model performance while RAG *helps* directly complicates the common assumption that adding more scaffolding (of any kind) is uniformly beneficial — the type of scaffold matters, not just its presence.

## Risks

- **Thin architectural/system-level coverage.** Only one source (TinyLLM, arxiv.org/pdf/2511.22138) directly addresses context management, memory systems, and sandboxed execution loops as a distinct category, and its full technical detail wasn't extractable from the fetched excerpt. This is the weakest-covered Scope category relative to the others.
- **RAG sources don't isolate small models.** The RACG survey and Meta-RAG paper both discuss retrieval-augmented code generation generally without differentiating by model scale; their relevance to small-model compensation specifically is inferred by this brief, not stated by the authors.
- **SWE-Compass granular breakdown unavailable.** Cataloged as a unified benchmark but the small-vs-large performance table could not be extracted from available content — treat step-6 claims about it as existence-only, not results-bearing.
- **No direct step-8 meta-analysis.** The consensus/disagreement narrative in Summary section 8 is this brief's own triangulation across four+ independently-sourced claims, not a single authoritative comparison. A future stage should treat this synthesis as a hypothesis to stress-test, not an established finding.
- **Single-source, unreviewed claims with striking numbers.** The 350M-model-beats-ChatGPT-CoT result (arxiv.org/abs/2512.15943) and the Devstral SWE-bench trajectory numbers (via the vendor's own announcement, only corroborated by an independent blog restating the same vendor figures rather than an independent measurement) both rest on results that have not been independently reproduced.
- **Vendor/product sources treated as supplementary.** distillabs.ai, morphllm.com, and pinggy.io are commercial or product-comparison blogs per the plan's own constraints; their claims are included as corroborating context, not as primary technical evidence, and should be weighted accordingly downstream.
- **Direct contradiction on self-verification scaffolds.** SWRV and CoCoS report self-verification/self-correction gains for small models, while the process-verification paper (arxiv.org/html/2601.00513) reports self-critique actively harms small-model performance. These are not fully reconcilable from the sourced material — possibly explained by different verification mechanisms (trained classifiers vs. free-form self-critique prompts) or different model/task scope, but this is not confirmed by any source and should be treated as an open tension, not resolved.
- **"State of the art" and benchmark-gap claims are time-sensitive and largely vendor- or single-paper-asserted.** Devstral's "SOTA among open code-agent models" claim originates from Mistral's own announcement; SWE-bench Pro's and SWE-EVO's sub-20%/25% figures come from single preprints each.

## Open Questions

- Does the "self-critique harms, RAG helps" finding (arxiv.org/html/2601.00513) generalize beyond the three 7–9B models it tested, and how does it reconcile with SWRV's and CoCoS's positive self-verification/self-correction results — is the difference mechanism (trained verifier vs. free-form self-critique), model scale, or task domain?
- Is live multi-small-model orchestration (ProST) or distillation of multi-agent behavior into a single small model (MapCoder-Lite) the more effective/efficient architecture for compute-constrained coding-agent deployment? No source directly compares the two.
- How much of Devstral's and similar models' reported SWE-bench gains are attributable to the underlying model versus the agentic scaffold (OpenHands, etc.) it's paired with, given the "scaffolding matters as much as scale" thread?
- Given SWE-bench Pro's and SWE-EVO's evidence that realistic/long-horizon tasks remain largely unsolved regardless of model size, how much of the "small models are closing the gap with large models" narrative (per the NVIDIA position paper) is scoped to easier, more saturated benchmarks (standard SWE-bench Verified, BFCL single-turn) versus harder, more realistic ones?
- What explains the sharp multi-turn tool-use collapse in very small models (e.g., Qwen3-0.6B dropping to 1.38% multi-turn per BFCL) — is this an architectural ceiling, a training-data gap, or a context-management limitation (tying back to the thin architectural/system-level source coverage)?
- Would a systematic, author-led comparison across technique categories (fine-tuning vs. RAG vs. scaffolding vs. multi-agent) change the triangulated consensus/disagreement picture assembled in this brief's Summary section 8, given none currently exists in the sourced material?
