# Counter-Evidence Plan: Larger-Scale Reach Channels for FellowScript's Leader/Pastor Audience

Task: `20260911-fellowscript-larger-scale-reach`

This plan scopes the critiquing stage's counter-evidence pass per `research-plan.md`'s own
instruction: "a counter-evidence pass that tries to knock down the strongest 2-3 'large-scale'
claims (the ones most likely to be vendor-inflated, e.g. RightNow Media's 4 million users, any
ChMS marketplace's install base, any podcast's download numbers)." The three targets below map
directly onto that instruction, selected because they are (a) the largest reach numbers in the
brief, (b) load-bearing for the brief's headline synthesis (Step 8), and (c) thinly or
self-corroborated per `sources.json`'s own `corroboration_count` and reliability notes.

## Target claims

**Claim 1 — RightNow Media's stated reach.**
> "RightNow Media's own Global Reach page states 140 countries, 30,000+ subscribing churches,
> 4.5M+ users, 25,000+ videos, 13 languages... The Wesleyan Church and the Learn of Christ review
> site repeat these figures, but both are reproductions of vendor copy, not independent
> measurement."

`corroboration_count: 3` in `sources.json`, but all three sources (RightNow's own page, a
denominational reproduction, a review-site reproduction) trace back to the same vendor claim —
this is repetition, not independent corroboration. It is also the brief's designated "single most
promising requires-leverage candidate," so an inflated or unverifiable figure would undercut a
headline recommendation, not just a minor data point.

**Claim 2 — Planning Center's install base and "actionable now" designation.**
> "Planning Center... 73,000 (Apps Run The World) vs. 100,000+ (faith.tools), with no current
> first-party figure... The sources support naming **Planning Center's open developer program** as
> the single best actionable-now candidate (free, self-serve, a listing path, and the largest
> install base in the ChMS set even at the lower 73K figure)."

This is the brief's single headline conclusion (the "best actionable-now candidate"), yet it rests
on (a) an install-base figure that is contested between two secondary sources, neither of which is
Planning Center's own current claim, and (b) a developer-program/directory-submission path sourced
only from Planning Center's own docs (`corroboration_count: 1`), with the brief's own Risks section
admitting "nothing in the sources says how many integrations are listed, how visible the directory
is to church staff, or whether a consumer-facing small-group app fits its intended scope." This is
the most load-bearing and least-corroborated claim in the brief.

**Claim 3 — Lifeway Leadership Podcast Network's monthly reach.**
> "Lifeway Leadership Podcast Network claims 200,000+ Christians, leaders, teams, and decision
> makers reached monthly... Listen Notes ranks network shows among the more popular of millions of
> podcasts, and one show... is cited at close to 2M cumulative downloads — corroborating the
> general reach claim in kind, though lifetime downloads are not the same unit as monthly reach."

`corroboration_count: 1` for the core 200,000+/month figure. The brief's own text flags that the
one piece of "corroboration" offered (2M cumulative downloads for one show) is a different metric
entirely and should not be read as confirming the vendor's monthly number — meaning this figure is
functionally uncorroborated despite reading as supported.

## Search angles

**Claim 1 (RightNow Media).** Genuine counter-evidence here is *independent measurement of actual
usage or install base*, not another site repeating the same press-kit numbers. Look for:
- App store data for RightNow Media's own consumer app(s) (iOS App Store / Google Play review
  counts, category rank, or any third-party app-intelligence estimate of installs/MAU) as an
  installed-base proxy independent of the vendor's own "4.5M users" framing — the same kind of
  proxy the sourcing stage already used for podcasts (Apple ratings counts) but not yet applied
  here.
- Denominational or church-tech trade press (ChurchTechToday, Church Executive, Outreach Magazine)
  running independent reporting or skepticism about RightNow Media's actual reach or pricing model,
  as opposed to denominational pages that just repeat the vendor's own copy verbatim (Wesleyan
  Church and Learn of Christ, already in `sources.json`, do not count as new evidence if
  re-surfaced).
- A sanity-check denominator: total number of churches/congregations in the US (e.g. Hartford
  Institute for Religion Research / Faith Communities Today survey figures, ~350,000-380,000 US
  congregations) to test whether "30,000+ subscribing churches" and "4.5M+ users" are
  arithmetically plausible relative to the total population of churches, rather than accepting the
  figure at face value.
- Direct search for the specific unreconciled discrepancy already flagged in the brief (30,000+ vs.
  20,000+ "partners") — does any source (investor material, a RightNow executive interview,
  conference talk, press release) explain the difference between the two figures, which would
  either validate or further undermine the headline number.

**Claim 2 (Planning Center).** Genuine counter-evidence is evidence bearing on either (a) the true
scale of Planning Center's install base, or (b) whether the developer/integrations-directory path
is a real distribution mechanism rather than an unused catalog. Look for:
- The same US-church-count sanity check as above, applied to Planning Center: is 73,000-100,000+
  churches plausible as a fraction of the total ~350,000-380,000 US congregations, especially
  compared against Pushpay's own vendor-stated 14,000+ and CCB's ~10,000 in the same brief?
- Market-sizing or analyst reports on the ChMS/church-software market (e.g. Grand View Research,
  MarketsandMarkets, or similar reports if they exist and are recent) that estimate total
  addressable churches or relative vendor market share — an independent framework rather than
  competing marketing claims.
- Review-platform signal (G2, Capterra, Software Advice) comparing Planning Center's review volume
  and reported customer counts against Pushpay's, CCB's, and ChurchTrac's, as a relative-scale
  cross-check rather than an absolute figure.
- Developer-community evidence on whether the integrations directory functions as real discovery:
  search developer forums, Reddit (e.g. r/ChurchTech if it exists), GitHub, or blog posts from
  third-party developers who have actually built and listed a Planning Center integration,
  describing whether it drove real church-admin/leader traffic or sat unused. This tests the
  brief's own open question directly rather than re-reading Planning Center's own docs again.
- Any funding, acquisition, or press-release material (Crunchbase, PitchBook profile, trade press
  on Planning Center as a company) that states a customer count from a source other than
  faith.tools or Apps Run The World.

**Claim 3 (Lifeway podcast network).** Genuine counter-evidence is independent podcast-analytics
data that can be compared against the 200,000+/month claim, not another Lifeway-controlled page.
Look for:
- Independent podcast analytics/ranking tools beyond Listen Notes (e.g. Podchaser, Rephonic,
  Chartable/Spotify or Apple Podcasts category charts) that give per-show estimated downloads or
  chart position for each show in the Lifeway Leadership Podcast Network, to build an independent
  aggregate estimate that can be compared to the network-wide claim — the same triangulation
  approach the sourcing stage already used for Rainer on Leadership (Feedspot range + Apple
  ratings), extended here across the whole network rather than one show.
- General trade-press or industry skepticism about podcast network "reach" figures as a genre (e.g.
  IAB/Podtrac industry benchmarks on typical downloads-to-unique-listener ratios, or
  advertising-industry commentary on inflated network-level claims), used to sanity-check whether
  200,000+/month is a plausible order of magnitude for a network of Lifeway's apparent size, not to
  find a specific rebuttal of Lifeway by name.
- Apple Podcasts ratings counts and Spotify/Apple chart category rankings for each individual show
  in the network (not just Group Answers and 5 Leadership Questions) as a circumstantial
  cross-check, consistent with the method already used elsewhere in this brief for
  Discipleship Leaders Podcast and Rainer on Leadership.

## Constraints

Inherited from `research-plan.md`:
- Prefer primary sources and independent corroboration (trade press, denominational reporting,
  traffic/analytics estimators) over vendor marketing copy alone; vendor-stated numbers are
  acceptable only when flagged as such.
- Flag anything older than ~3 years as potentially stale.
- Attempt direct platform fetches (App Store, Google Play, podcast platforms) where possible before
  falling back to third-party estimators or search snippets; document precisely if a fetch is
  blocked rather than silently dropping it.
- Do not re-litigate settled scope: this pass stays within RightNow Media, ChMS marketplaces, and
  podcast reach — it does not reopen faith.tools, SGN's conferences, leader-first vs. institutional
  strategy, or paid-ads strategy (all out of bounds per `research-plan.md`).

Specific to counter-evidence:
- A source that merely repeats the same vendor claim in different words (another denominational
  page copying RightNow's numbers, another directory site copying Planning Center's or Pushpay's
  homepage tagline) does not count as counter-evidence and should not be logged as if it moves the
  needle — the sourcing stage already established that pattern for all three claims.
- Prioritize sources with their own methodology (app-intelligence estimators, traffic estimators,
  market-sizing reports, independent podcast analytics, review-platform aggregates) or their own
  independent reporting (trade press, developer first-hand accounts) over anything that is simply
  restating a headline number.
- Where a genuine sanity-check denominator exists (total US churches, total podcast-industry
  download-to-listener ratios), use it explicitly rather than treating each vendor figure in
  isolation.
- This is a good-faith counter-evidence effort, not a demolition assignment: if independent data
  for a claim turns out to corroborate the vendor figure (e.g. an app-store install estimate that
  actually supports RightNow Media's user count), report that honestly rather than continuing to
  search until something negative surfaces.

## Success criteria

A good-faith counter-evidence effort for this topic looks like, for each of the three target
claims:
- At least one source was checked that has its own independent methodology or first-hand account
  (not a repetition of the vendor's own figure) — e.g. an app-store/analytics proxy, a market-sizing
  or denominator sanity-check, a developer's first-hand account of the Planning Center directory, or
  independent podcast-analytics data.
- The result is reported honestly whether it weakens, leaves unchanged, or (rarely) strengthens
  confidence in the claim — not selectively kept only if it's negative.
- For Claim 2 specifically (the brief's headline "best actionable-now" designation), the pass
  should reach at least a documented attempt to test whether the integrations directory is a real,
  used distribution channel, since that is the single most load-bearing and least-corroborated
  claim in the brief.
- Where no genuine independent data can be found for a claim despite a real attempt (as already
  happened once in sourcing for the RightNow Media sponsorship-mechanism question), that absence
  should be documented precisely — which independent source types were checked and came up empty —
  rather than substituting more vendor-adjacent repetition and calling it corroboration.
