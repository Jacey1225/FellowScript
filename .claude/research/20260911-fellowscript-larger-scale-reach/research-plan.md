# Research Plan: Larger-Scale Reach Channels for FellowScript's Leader/Pastor Audience

Task: `20260911-fellowscript-larger-scale-reach`
Part 2 of a deliberate two-part sequence. Builds directly on
`.claude/research/20260911-fellowscript-leader-identify-reach-pitch/conclusion.md` (Part 1).
Does not restart identification/pitch work already settled there.

## Topic / Claim

Part 1 identified concrete but small-scale/high-touch channels for reaching FellowScript's
target persona (church small-group leaders, i.e. SGPPs, and pastors who oversee small groups):
a niche directory listing (faith.tools, ranked ~705,000 globally, subscriber counts in the
hundreds) and in-person conference attendance (Small Group Network's ALIGN/ACCELERATE, $124-$209
per person, inherently capped by physical attendance). Part 1 explicitly flagged these as
relatively small-scale and named RightNow Media's 30,000+ churches / 4M users as a channel
mentioned in sourcing but never fully explored.

This run's claim to investigate: **there exist alternative distribution channels, partnerships,
platforms, or content strategies that can reach this same persona at meaningfully larger scale
than a small directory listing or a two-day conference — and some subset of those are actually
actionable by a small team (one or two people) without requiring partnership leverage FellowScript
does not yet have.**

The job is not just to list large-reach channels, but to sort them into (a) actionable now for a
small unfunded/lean team, and (b) large in theoretical reach but gated behind partnership access,
budget, or scale FellowScript doesn't currently have — and to be honest when a channel falls in
the second bucket rather than dressing it up as achievable.

## Scope

**In bounds:**
- Distribution channels/partnerships/platforms with a documented reach number (subscriber count,
  install base, member count, audience size, church count, etc.) serving church small-group
  leaders and pastors specifically (or a clearly-adjacent, disproportionately relevant subset of
  a broader Christian/church audience).
- Categories to investigate at minimum (per the request, non-exhaustive):
  - Curriculum publishers with distribution/reach beyond content sales (e.g. RightNow Media,
    Lifeway, The Gospel Coalition, LifeGroups content ecosystems) — full exploration of
    RightNow Media as a channel specifically, since Part 1 only sourced its customer-count claim
    and left "does it accept third-party content in its monthly leader email" as an open question.
  - Denominational networks/associations (national or cross-church bodies with real membership
    numbers) distinct from the single-congregation LifeGroups.com correction already made in
    Part 1.
  - Church-management-software (ChMS) integrations, app marketplaces, or partner directories
    (e.g. Planning Center, Breeze, ChurchTrac, Church Community Builder, Pushpay/Subsplash
    ecosystems) — is there an app-marketplace or integration-partner model with reach numbers,
    and what does it take to get listed/integrated.
  - Podcasts and YouTube channels serving small-group leaders/pastors specifically, with
    subscriber/download numbers, and whether they take sponsorships, guest content, or reviews at
    a price point a small team could afford.
  - Facebook groups and other online communities — Part 1 left this step (Step 2) entirely
    unresolved for lack of browser tooling; this run should attempt to close that gap with real
    member-count/activity data where retrievable, or document precisely why it remains
    unretrievable.
  - Any other large-reach channel that surfaces during sourcing and plausibly reaches this
    persona (e.g. large single-church podcasts/blogs with cross-church readership, ministry
    conferences larger than Small Group Network's, App Store/Play Store faith-category placement
    dynamics, newsletter sponsorships aimed at ministry staff).
- For every channel identified: reach numbers (with source and caveats about reliability), cost
  or access requirements, and an explicit actionable-now vs. requires-leverage-not-yet-held
  classification.

**Out of bounds (already settled, do not re-litigate):**
- Whether to pursue leader-first vs. institutional/diocese-partnership strategy — already decided
  in favor of leader-first in `20260815-fellowscript-outreach-tactics`. Channels here are
  evaluated as ways to reach *leaders/pastors themselves*, not as a reopening of the
  institutional-partnership question. A channel that only works by first striking a formal
  institutional partnership deal should be flagged as exactly that kind of leverage gap, not
  recommended as if it were direct-to-leader.
- Re-deriving how to identify individual leaders (job-board title-watching, etc.) — settled in
  Part 1's Step 1.
- Re-evaluating faith.tools or Small Group Network's ALIGN/ACCELERATE conferences as channels —
  Part 1 already assessed these; this run only references them as the small-scale baseline to
  beat, not as fresh research targets.
- Pitch messaging, objection-handling, and timing (Part 1 Steps 5-8) — out of scope here unless a
  new channel surfaces a genuinely new objection or timing consideration specific to that channel
  (e.g. sponsorship lead times for a podcast).
- Paid-ads-driven acquisition strategy in general — already deprioritized vs. referral growth in
  `20260814-fellowscript-marketing`; a channel that is fundamentally "buy paid ads here" should be
  evaluated only insofar as it's a distribution/placement mechanism with an organic or
  low-cost on-ramp (e.g. free directory/marketplace listing), not as a case for a paid-acquisition
  pivot.
- Consumer-facing mass-market channels that reach end-users/congregants rather than the
  leader/pastor decision-maker persona specifically (e.g. general Christian content going viral
  with congregants) — out of scope unless the channel demonstrably reaches leaders/pastors as a
  distinct, identifiable segment of its audience.

## Constraints

- No time period restriction on the underlying channels/platforms themselves, but reach figures
  should be as current as available (flag anything older than ~3 years as potentially stale, per
  Part 1's own practice with its 2011/2013 timing sources).
- Source types: prefer primary sources (the platform/publisher/network's own stated reach numbers,
  App Store/Play Store data, traffic estimators like Similarweb) and independent corroboration
  (trade press, denominational reporting) over vendor marketing copy alone. Vendor-stated numbers
  are acceptable but must be flagged as vendor-stated and, where possible, cross-checked the way
  Part 1 cross-checked ChurchTrac/RightNow Media figures.
  Facebook/Reddit group data should be attempted directly; if automated fetching is blocked (as
  it was in Part 1), document that limitation precisely rather than silently dropping the step.
- Depth expected: comparable to Part 1 — a sourcing brief with named sources per claim, a
  counter-evidence pass that tries to knock down the strongest 2-3 "large-scale" claims (the ones
  most likely to be vendor-inflated, e.g. RightNow Media's 4 million users, any ChMS marketplace's
  install base, any podcast's download numbers), and a final conclusion that explicitly separates
  actionable-now from requires-leverage-not-yet-held.
- This run must reference and build on Part 1's conclusion by name where relevant (e.g. explicitly
  revisiting the RightNow Media open question) rather than re-deriving already-settled findings
  from scratch.

## Research steps

1. **RightNow Media as a channel, fully explored.** What is the actual, current reach (30,000+
   churches / 4 million users — verify, don't just repeat) and does it have any documented
   mechanism (sponsored content, guest webinar, marketing partnership, its monthly leader email)
   through which a third-party app could reach its leader audience? What would that access cost
   or require, and is it plausible for a small team?
2. **Denominational and cross-church networks at scale.** Beyond the single-congregation
   LifeGroups.com correction already made, are there real cross-church denominational bodies
   (e.g. specific denominations' national small-group/discipleship offices, parachurch
   discipleship networks) with a documented number of member churches or leaders reachable
   through a newsletter, conference, or resource-sharing program — and what access do they permit
   external vendors?
3. **Curriculum publishers beyond RightNow Media.** Lifeway, The Gospel Coalition, and any other
   major small-group curriculum publisher named in Part 1's plan but never sourced there — do
   they have documented audience size, and any sponsorship/advertising/guest-content mechanism?
4. **Church-management-software (ChMS) marketplaces/integrations.** Do major ChMS platforms
   (Planning Center, Breeze, ChurchTrac, Church Community Builder, Subsplash, Pushpay, etc.) run
   an app marketplace, integration-partner program, or co-marketing channel with a stated
   install-base/customer count, and what does it take (technical integration, partnership deal,
   fee) for a third-party app like FellowScript to get listed or featured?
5. **Podcasts and YouTube channels serving this persona.** Which podcasts/YouTube channels are
   made for or disproportionately consumed by small-group leaders and pastors (distinct from
   general Christian content), what are their documented audience sizes, and do they take
   sponsorships, interviews, or app reviews — at what cost?
6. **Facebook groups and online communities, revisited.** Attempt to retrieve actual member counts
   and activity levels for the named Facebook groups (Small Group Network's group and any others
   Katie Allred's list surfaces) and any other online community (subreddit, Discord, Slack) for
   this persona. If still blocked, document the specific blocker rather than leaving it silently
   unresolved again.
7. **App Store / Play Store faith-category placement.** Is there a scalable organic-discovery
   mechanism (category rankings, featured placement, ASO within the faith/Bible-study category)
   that reaches this persona at meaningfully larger scale than a directory listing, and what does
   reaching it actually require?
8. **Synthesis: actionable-now vs. requires-leverage.** For every channel found across steps 1-7,
   classify it explicitly: reach number (with reliability caveat), cost/requirement to access, and
   whether a 1-2 person team can act on it now or whether it requires partnership leverage,
   capital, or scale FellowScript does not currently have. Name the single best actionable-now
   candidate and the single most promising requires-leverage candidate worth working toward.
