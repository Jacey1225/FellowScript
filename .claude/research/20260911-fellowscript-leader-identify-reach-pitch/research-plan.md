# Research Plan: Identifying, Reaching, and Pitching FellowScript to Small-Group Leaders/Pastors

## Topic / Claim

FellowScript's primary adoption decision-makers are church small-group leaders and pastors — one
leader's buy-in unlocks a whole group (up to 8 members) under the group-priced subscription model.
Given that, what are the most effective, specific ways to:

(a) **Identify** who these small-group leaders/pastors actually are (where they cluster online and
    offline, what makes someone identifiable as "a small-group leader" vs. just an active
    congregant, what directories/lists/signals exist to find them at scale),
(b) **Reach** them (which specific channels, communities, platforms, events, or intermediaries
    put a solo marketer/small team in front of these people, and how, mechanically), and
(c) **Pitch** FellowScript to them specifically (what messaging, framing, offer structure, and
    proof points move a small-group leader or pastor from aware to piloting with their group).

This is a continuation of, not a re-run of, two prior `/research` tasks. It should go deeper and
more concrete/tactical on identification, reach, and pitch mechanics for group leaders
specifically — the layer of specificity neither prior run fully resolved.

## Scope

**In bounds:**
- Concrete identification tactics: what directories, platforms, hashtags, LinkedIn/Facebook group
  search patterns, church-staff-directory conventions, denominational small-group networks,
  curriculum-publisher customer lists, conference attendee lists, etc. make small-group
  leaders/pastors findable as a definable, targetable population (not just "small-group leaders
  exist" — actual mechanics of finding named individuals or reachable clusters of them)
- Concrete reach tactics: specific platforms/communities/newsletters/podcasts/Facebook or Discord
  groups/subreddits/conferences/curriculum ecosystems (e.g. LifeGroups, Right Now Media,
  Lifeway, Small Group Network, Rightnow Media, church-tech blogs) where small-group leaders and
  pastors already gather, and how a small team could plausibly show up there (cost, access
  barriers, gatekeeping)
- Concrete pitch tactics: messaging angles, objection-handling, offer structuring (free
  trial/pilot terms), proof-point types (testimonials, demo format, first-meeting onboarding
  script elements) that are documented to move this specific persona type (small-group/Bible
  study leader, lay or clergy) from interested to adopting
- Explicitly named open items from the prior two runs that bear directly on this question:
  no faith-specific leader-first precedent found yet; no literal cold-outreach script/DM template
  found yet; ministry-coordinator pitch angles undiscovered; true shape of pitch-vs-compliance
  material for gatekeepers unresolved for the US context
- Adjacent/analogous domains (EdTech teacher-first adoption, community-led growth, small-team
  SaaS motions) may be used for pitch/reach mechanics *specifically when* no faith-specific
  source exists, exactly as the prior task did — but any such analogy must be flagged as
  cross-domain borrowing, not faith-specific precedent

**Out of bounds (already resolved, don't re-derive):**
- Whether to prioritize referral/group-invite growth vs. paid acquisition (resolved: referral-first)
- Whether to prioritize leader-first vs. diocese/institutional-first outreach (resolved: leader-first)
- Whether to pursue campus-ministry coalitions (resolved: defer)
- General ASO tactics, general monetization/pricing questions, general comparable-app
  positioning (Hallow/YouVersion/Abide/SmartGroups) — already covered in
  `20260814-fellowscript-marketing`
- Re-litigating the debunked stats (83% referral trust, 30% Catholic-partnership increase,
  February 2024 Hallow calendar-timing causation) — treat these as settled-false, don't resurrect
- Diocese/parish-administrator-specific persuasion material — flagged unresolved by prior research
  but explicitly deprioritized since FellowScript is going leader-first, not institution-first

## Constraints

- No time period restriction, but prefer material from the last ~5 years for platform/channel
  specifics (church-tech and small-group-network landscape shifts quickly); older sourcing is
  fine for durable behavioral/psychology claims (e.g. adoption-pattern research)
- Source types: general web/practitioner sources are acceptable (as in both prior runs), but
  every load-bearing statistic or "this tactic works" claim must be traceable to a specific,
  named, checkable source — no restating unsourced marketing-blog claims as fact
- Depth expected: tactical/operational specificity is the point of this run — vague restatement
  of "leader-first outreach works" (already concluded) is not a sufficient answer; the bar is
  actual channel names, actual identification mechanics, actual pitch language/structure
  documented somewhere, or a clearly labeled reasoned inference where no direct source exists
- Do not fabricate faith-specific precedent where none exists — if a faith-specific analog isn't
  found, say so explicitly, exactly as the prior run did, rather than overstating an analogy

## Research steps

1. **Identification mechanics**: What concrete signals, directories, platforms, or public
   information sources let someone identify named small-group leaders and pastors of small/
   independent/non-denominational churches (FellowScript's inferred audience) at scale, as
   opposed to identifying churches or denominations generally?
2. **Where leaders self-organize online**: What specific online communities (Facebook groups,
   subreddits, Discord servers, LinkedIn groups, forums) are documented to be populated by small-
   group leaders/pastors specifically (not congregants generally), and what's the actual size/
   activity level/access model of each?
3. **Curriculum and small-group-network ecosystems**: What role do small-group curriculum
   publishers and networks (e.g. Lifeway, RightNow Media, Small Group Network, The Gospel
   Coalition small-groups resources, LifeGroups.com, church-specific small-group platforms) play
   as both an identification signal (their customer/user bases are self-selected small-group
   leaders) and a potential reach channel (co-marketing, guest content, directory listing)?
4. **Conferences and events**: What specific conferences, retreats, or training events are
   attended specifically by small-group pastors/leaders (not general church staff), what do they
   cost to attend/exhibit at, and is this documented as a viable reach channel for a small team
   (building on the prior run's "champion-sourcing through conference networking" finding, which
   was asserted but not made concrete)?
5. **Direct-outreach mechanics and scripts**: Does any documented source provide an actual cold-
   outreach template, DM script, or first-contact framework aimed at a pastor or small-group
   leader (the gap both prior runs explicitly flagged as unfilled)? If genuinely none exists,
   what's the closest analogous template (EdTech teacher-outreach, SaaS champion-outreach) that
   could be adapted, clearly labeled as adapted rather than faith-specific?
6. **Pitch content and messaging angles**: What specific messaging, framing, or value-proposition
   language is documented to resonate with small-group/Bible-study leaders considering a new
   tool — e.g. time-savings for leaders, deepening group engagement, replacing scattered tools
   (group texts, paper notes), objections around cost/data-privacy/screen-time-in-church-context?
7. **Objection handling and proof points**: What objections are documented as common blockers
   for this persona (cost to the leader when the group doesn't split it, tech-literacy of older
   congregants, doctrinal/content-safety concerns, "another app" fatigue), and what proof-point
   formats (live demo at first meeting, testimonial type, free-pilot terms) are shown to overcome
   them?
8. **Ministry-coordinator angle** (a named gap from the prior run): Is there any documented
   pitch angle or identification path for a "ministry coordinator" role distinct from lead pastor
   or small-group leader, or does the prior finding (this role implements rather than decides)
   hold up under a dedicated look?
